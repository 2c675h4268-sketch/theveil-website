What Changed

The story this week is a bureaucratic one, and that is the point. The Pentagon's fifth tranche of UAP files (CBS at 90, NBC at 82) turns disclosure into a monthly habit rather than a single event. Five drops in, the routine itself matters more than any one clip. The real test is whether any file carries data specific enough for outside analysts to work with, instead of renderings and clips no one can resolve. So far the documentary standouts are the checkable ones: that classified 1953 Navy analysis in the PURSUE records, and the FBI file describing a named former Pentagon employee reporting a translucent triangle over Colorado in 2023.

Watch two things. Whether the Air Force actually releases those two withheld triangle videos with a date, not an open-ended "review." And whether Loeb's science council publishes real analysis rather than commentary.

Discount the Grusch claim that Cheney oversaw an alien "detainment" (Post, 42): a podcast assertion naming a dead man, no documents. The jumped-watch anecdote and RT's German "surge" are attention artifacts until someone cites the source.
